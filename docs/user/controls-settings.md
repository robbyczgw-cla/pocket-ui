# Controls in Settings, setup and on the touch screen

The second half of [Controls](controls.md): the buttons in Settings and its pages, during setup, on the touch screen,
and the power and volume keys. The Mini V2's picture, the shelf, games, the pause menu and Options are on
[the first page](controls.md).

## In Settings

Settings opens on seven categories (Look, Sound & feel, Playing, Games, Connections, System, Help & tools); each holds a short list
([Open Settings](settings.md#open-settings)).

Connections holds Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, Tailscale, Remote access (SSH) and Web companion, where the device supports them.
Use Up/Down to select either network tool, A to open it, and B to return to its row in Connections.
Sync and PC streaming are under Help & tools → For tinkerers.

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| D-pad Up / Down | Move. The list wraps around at the ends. |
| HOME | Back to Home from wherever you are in Settings (the categories, a list, About, Battery, Lights, Night light, Cozy extras, Sleep & power, Curator). Pages with something under way (an update or a download, a sign-in, a pairing, a clean-up, a button being remapped) keep it: B goes back there. On Home and the shelf HOME still opens the quick settings. |
| A | On a category: open its list. In the list: open. On Screen colour: back to Neutral. On Appearance, Colours, Style, Text size, Motion, Zoom in the pause menu, Animations, Sounds, Haptics, On start, Continue where I quit, Remind me where I was, Over the game, Screen frames and Language: switch. On Buttons: open its page. |
| D-pad Left / Right | On Sounds: how loud. On Screen colour, Appearance, Colours, Style, Text size, Motion, Zoom in the pause menu, Animations, On start, Over the game, Screen frames and Language: switch. On the Buttons page: A or Left / Right switch the selected row, Button layout or Button symbols. Controller setup opens the separate layout for each connected controller. |
| B | In a category's list: back to the categories, the one you came from selected. On the categories: back to where you came from (the shelf, or a system's game list). |

A page of Settings (Wi-Fi, Tools, Battery and the others below) goes back with B to its category's list, even when
it was opened from somewhere else.

## Touch screen

The touch screen works next to the buttons; it never does anything a button can't.

On the shelf, the game lists, Settings, the search and Recent games:

| Touch | What it does |
|---|---|
| Tap a row (or card) | Select it. Tap the selected one again to open or play it. |
| Swipe up / down | Scroll the list: the selection follows the finger by whole rows. The list stops at its ends. In the manual the page follows the finger. |
| Swipe left / right | In Recent games: previous / next card. In the Cabinet: along the shelf (up / down: to another shelf). |
| Tap a button hint at the bottom (A, B, X, Y, SELECT, START) | The same as pressing that button. START opens Settings. |
| Tap a key of the search's keyboard | Type it. |
| Tap a Curator row on a game's card (Echoes, Versions, Stickers) | Select it. Tap it again to open it. |

In a game, pocket-ui ignores the touch screen and the game gets it:
- **Nintendo DS**: touch the bottom screen with a finger as you would with the stylus. It follows the layout in use
  (one above the other, side by side). Touching the top screen, or anywhere beside the picture, does nothing.
  The right stick and R2 still work as a pointer, if you prefer them.
- Other systems ignore it.

The quick settings and the power menu can be touched, even over a paused game: a row selects it, the selected
one does what A does, the arrows round a value are Left and Right, and the hints at the bottom are their buttons.
The pause menu and the first-start setup are button-only for now. Touching the screen keeps the device awake (it
counts like a button), but a touch never wakes a sleeping screen.

If the touch screen does not answer: pocket-ui looks for it until it is there, and opens it again if it was lost
(after a restart of the touch driver, say), so it comes back by itself. Touches are also dropped on purpose while a game
or app runs, while the screen is off or the Pocket sleeps (only a button wakes it), on the pause menu and the first-start
setup, and while the picture is on a TV. Which one it was is in the log (`/var/log/pocket-ui.log`, lines starting
`touch:`); Settings → Help & tools → About → Report a problem includes it.

## Power and volume

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| Power, short press | Sleep. In a game, the game is saved first and goes on where it was when you wake the device. Press Power again to wake it. Or, when set so, **Screen off**: only the screen goes dark and the game goes on. |
| Power, held for about a second | Opens the **power menu**. Letting go after that does not also put the device to sleep. Or, when set so, **Sleep** when you let go. The **Power…** row of the [quick settings](quick-settings.md#quick-settings) opens the same menu; B there goes back to the quick settings. |
| Power, held for many seconds | The device's own forced reset, without saving. pocket-ui leaves it alone: use it only when nothing responds. |
| Vol+ / Vol- | Volume (H700 speaker: reduced maximum and physical mute at zero). With START held: brightness. With SELECT held: colour temperature. |

Sleep starts when you let go of the button, not when you press it: that is how a short press is told
from a held one. A short press never opens a menu, and the press that wakes the device does nothing else.

What the short press and the hold do is set in Settings → System → **Sleep & power**
([Sleep & power settings](quick-settings.md#sleep--power-settings)).

### Volume and brightness keys

These work on the shelf, in quick settings and in games. A bar at the top of the screen shows the level.

| Keys | What it does |
|---|---|
| Vol+ / Vol- | Volume |
| START + Vol+ / Vol- | Brightness, in steps of 5%, never below 5% |
| HOME + Vol+ / Vol- | Brightness (ROCKNIX does the change, pocket-ui shows the bar) |
| SELECT + Vol+ / Vol- | Colour: Vol+ goes warmer (more yellow-red), Vol- goes cooler (more blue). The bar shows **Cooler 1…10**, **Neutral** or **Warmer 1…10**; if the screen looks yellowish, press SELECT + Vol- a few times. The same setting as Settings → Look → **Screen colour** (Left/Right there, A for Neutral) |

RetroArch games and the standalone emulators get no bar; the changes still apply. The colour temperature reaches
RetroArch, PlayStation 2, GameCube and PortMaster games only when the screen supports it system-wide.

### The power menu

At the top right: the battery level and the time left, as on Settings → Playing → [Battery](settings-system.md#battery).
Under each entry, a line says what it does.

| Entry | What it does |
|---|---|
| Sleep | Saves the running game and puts the Pocket to sleep (the same as the default short press). |
| Restart | Saves the running game, then restarts the device. pocket-ui comes back and opens the game again. |
| Power off | Saves the running game, then switches the device off. |
| Restart pocket-ui | Saves and closes the running game, then starts only pocket-ui again, in a few seconds; the system keeps running. pocket-ui comes back and opens the game again. For when a screen seems stuck, without restarting the whole device. |
| Restart to boot menu | Restarts into the bootloader's menu, where you can switch the boot mode to Android (choose **Switch boot mode**, then Android, then **Start**). Only shown when the device can do that. Without it, the menu says how to get to Android by hand: Power off, then hold Vol Down + Power and choose Switch boot mode. |

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| D-pad Up / Down | Move. |
| A | Choose. |
| B, or the Power button again | Close the menu. In a game, the game goes on where it was. |

In a game, the menu pauses it first, like the pause menu does (the pause menu is open behind it).
Closing the menu puts the game back; choosing Sleep puts it back and then sleeps, so the game goes on
after waking.

For Wild Arms XF and Crisis Core in pocket-ui's own PSP host, GPU readbacks are skipped by
default to avoid an exit-save hang. An explicit Emulator option saved for the game or console
wins over this default. If effects look wrong, change the readback option in Emulator options.

### Test buttons

**Settings → Help & tools → Test buttons** lights up every button, trigger and stick of each connected controller and
says what the last press does in menus and in games after your layout. A handheld without sticks (the TrimUI Brick, the RG35XX Plus)
is drawn without them. Hold HOME for 2 seconds (or B and START
together) to leave. See [Test buttons](settings-system.md#test-buttons).

### Controller setup

Open **Settings → Playing → Buttons → Controller setup**, or **Pause → Options → Controls → Controller setup**
in a pocket-core game. The first row chooses the controller; its identity suffix distinguishes two pads with the
same name, and its player number shows the remembered order. A or Left/Right switches the controller.

Choose an action, press A, then press the wanted button on that controller. Analogue L2/R2 are captured too.
A button already assigned to another action **swaps** with the previous button; a note confirms the swap.
While it waits, the page says which controller to press. HOME on any controller cancels, and so does any
button of the handheld's own controls while you set up another controller; with no press for 8 seconds it gives
up by itself and nothing changes. Input from another controller cannot change the capture. B leaves the page;
**Reset controller layout** restores only the selected controller.

On the TrimUI Brick, F1/F2 are reserved shortcuts. Pressing one during capture ends it with an explanation;
no game button changes. Choose their global shortcut actions in Settings → Sound & feel → **Lights & buttons**.

If the selected controller disconnects while the page waits, capture ends without changing the layout.
Reconnect it and choose the action again. Held buttons are released when its input reader is replaced too,
so a silent old connection cannot keep a direction or trigger held in the menus or game.

The layout is saved automatically for the controller's identity and follows it through reconnects and player
changes. It applies before the Nintendo/Xbox menu layout and before each game's Controls settings: the shelf,
menus, pocket-core games and ROCKNIX's SDL Dolphin and ARMSX2 use it. RetroArch, Azahar (3DS), PortMaster and other external players keep their own controller setup.

Trigger press is at 55% travel; release is below 40%, preventing repeated presses near the threshold. A digital
trigger behaves like an ordinary button. Unchanged analogue triggers keep their travel in standalone games. A remapped trigger
uses the same press/release thresholds as the menus. HOME, power and volume remain available for navigation and recovery.

On the TrimUI Brick, volume keys while the mute slider is engaged show **Muted** and keep the
previous level. Release the slider to return to that exact level.

Game and system names keep their original wording in every interface language, including wrapped cover and card labels.
