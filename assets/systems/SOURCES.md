# System pictures: sources and licences

Each system card on the shelf shows a pixel-art picture of the console (`<system id>.png`, compiled into
pocket-ui by `src/system_art.rs`). The pictures show the hardware only so that the system can be recognised
at a glance. Every logo, brand name and product name printed on the hardware or its controller has been
painted over, so the pictures carry no trademarks. Names such as "Game Boy" or "Mega Drive" belong to their
owners and appear in pocket-ui only as plain text labels.

The first Dreamcast choice, `Dreamcast-Console-Set.jpg`, is CC BY-SA 3.0 on Commons, so the public-domain
`Sega-Dreamcast-Console-FL.jpg` (console without controller) was used instead.

The pictures for `wii` (the console standing upright, with a Wiimote and its strap) and `3ds` (the original
Nintendo 3DS, opened) cover the aliases `wiiware` and `n3ds` as well. The 3ds photo carries no logo, only the
labels SELECT, HOME, START, POWER and OFF, which were painted over because the pixel conversion turned them
into noise. On the Wii photo the white Wiimote stands in front of the white console, so the object mask
misses the top of the remote: `tools/cutout.py` was run with `--fill 505,262,665,320` to keep it. The Wii
and Nintendo logos, the ATI logo, the POWER, RESET and HOME labels and the "Wii" lettering on the console and
on the Wiimote were removed.

Fifteen pictures were added on 2026-10-27 the same way: `segacd` (the Model 1, the Mega Drive with the CD unit
under it; it also stands for the `megacd` folder), `sega32x` (the 32X on a Model 2 Mega Drive), `pcenginecd` (the
TurboGrafx-CD with its interface unit; also `tg16cd`), `neogeo` (the AES console), `neocd` (the top-loader with its
pad), `atarilynx`, `atari7800`, `ngpc` (also the `ngp` folder, as the WonderSwan picture covers the WonderSwan
Color), `virtualboy`, `atarijaguar`, `3do` (the GoldStar model with its pad), `fds` (the Disk System under a
Famicom), `colecovision`, `intellivision` and `vectrex`. A Sega CD picture made a little earlier on this machine
without PixelLab was replaced by a PixelLab conversion, so it matches the others. Some of Evan Amos's photos of these
consoles are CC BY-SA 3.0 on Commons (the "Console-Set" photos of the 7800, Jaguar, Intellivision and Vectrex, and the
JPEG copies of the Virtual Boy and 3DO photos); in each case the public-domain file in the table was taken instead.
The two PNG photos (`3do`, `virtualboy`) have a transparent background; it was filled with white before step 1. The
lettering removed: SEGA, GENESIS, 16-BIT and the red band on the Sega CD lid, the SEGA CD and SEGA marks on its tray;
SUPER 32X and SEGA; the TurboGrafx logo and NEC labels; NEO GEO, SNK and the AES/CD lettering; LYNX, ATARI and the
orange button labels on the Lynx; the Atari 7800 logo; NEO GEO POCKET COLOR and SNK; VIRTUAL BOY and Nintendo on
the visor, the pad and the stand; JAGUAR and ATARI; 3DO and GoldStar; FAMILY COMPUTER, Nintendo and the Disk System
lettering; COLECOVISION and the Coleco badge; INTELLIVISION / Mattel Electronics; the Vectrex lettering. For
slanted strips of lettering `tools/fix.py` takes a polygon (`poly`) instead of a box.

What was changed in every picture above:

1. **Crop.** The photo was cropped to the object with a 3 % margin and scaled to 1280 px on its long side
   (`tools/prep.py`). The nes, snes, psx and n64 photos were used uncropped.
2. **Pixel-art conversion.** PixelLab `image-to-pixelart`, output a fifth of the input (about 256 px on the
   long side). The settings are in `pixellab-assets.json`.
3. **Background removed.** A flood fill from the image border, guarded by an object mask taken from the photo
   so that white plastic is not cut away, followed by removal of the light edge left by the photo backdrop
   (`tools/cutout.py`, `tools/fix.py`).
4. **Logos and brand text removed.** Every logo, brand name, product name and model lettering on the
   console and the controller was repainted with the surrounding plastic colour (`tools/fix.py`, with one
   entry per area in `tools/cleanup-ops.json`).
5. **Stored** as a lossless palette PNG with on/off transparency (`tools/finalize.py`).

Plain function labels such as START, SELECT, POWER and the A/B button letters were left where they are
too small to read. The coloured face buttons of the PlayStation-family controllers keep their colours.

## Generated from text (SuperGrafx, Pokémon mini)

Commons has no public-domain photo of these two (Evan Amos's photos of the SuperGrafx are CC BY-SA 3.0, the others
CC BY or CC BY-SA; of the Pokémon mini there are only CC BY-SA photos), and a CC BY-SA photo converted to pixels would
bring its share-alike condition into the pictures. So `supergrafx.png` and `pokemini.png` are **generated from text**:
PixelLab `create-image-pixflux` (v2) from a text prompt alone, **no photo and no input image of any kind**, on
2026-10-27: transparent background asked for, `single color black outline`, `medium shading`, `medium detail`, view
`high top-down`, direction `south`, text guidance 8. Eleven requests in all (5 for the Pokémon mini and 6 for the
SuperGrafx, one generation each, 11 credits from the "reserve" row of the shared budget); the two that were used are in
the table, every request with its prompt and seed is in `pixellab-assets.json` (keys `t_sg_*`, `t_pm_*`, `t_pmL_*`).
The first three Pokémon mini requests (192x160, a blue handheld) came out only 134 px big, below the 150 px the
pictures' test wants, so it was asked again on a 256x224 canvas.

| File | Prompt | Seed | Size asked | Request |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `supergrafx.png` | a wide low black plastic retro home video game console, boxy with slightly sloped top, a horizontal card slot on the top, seen from the front at a three-quarter angle, next to a small black rectangular game pad with a cross d-pad, two red round buttons and a cable plugged into the console, matte dark charcoal, no logo, no text, no letters | 15 | 256x160 | `t_sg_15` |
| `pokemini.png` | a tiny rounded pocket handheld game console in translucent blue plastic, small grey LCD screen on the upper part, a small cross d-pad on the left, two small round buttons on the right, a small cartridge slot on top, seen from the front slightly from above, no logo, no text, no letters, single object | 24 | 256x224 | `t_pmL_24` |

What was changed (`tools/text_art.py make`): both came back on an opaque white backdrop (the SuperGrafx with a light grey
drop shadow as well), which a flood fill from the border removed. The SuperGrafx: the lettering the generator had
invented on the console's lid (a white word and a red one, and red specks) and the white inside the cable's loop were
repainted with the surrounding plastic colour, and it got the one-pixel light rim by name (below). The Pokémon mini
needed nothing else; it has no lettering. Both were stored as lossless palette PNGs.

What they are and are not: these are pictures *in the style of* the others (one-pixel dark outline, flat shading, about
25 to 65 colours), **not** pictures of the real hardware. The SuperGrafx is a generic black console with a card slot
and a pad with two red buttons; the real console's shape and its pad differ in the details. The Pokémon mini is a
generic light-blue handheld with a screen, a D-pad and two buttons (it looks more like a Game Boy than like the real
thing); it carries no Pokémon artwork and no lettering. They are there so that the two systems are recognised as "a
console" and "a handheld" on the shelf. Neither shows a logo or a brand name; the names "SuperGrafx" and "Pokémon
mini" appear in pocket-ui only as text.

Licence: PixelLab's terms (see `NOTICE.md`) give the user the copyright in what is created; with no photo behind
them, nothing of Evan Amos's or anyone else's is in these two. They ship under GPL-2.0-or-later like the others.

## Rim for pictures that are dark all over

Pictures of which 85 % or more of the pixels are nearly as dark as the spine (#1F1F22) get a one pixel light
grey-blue rim (#8C93A3) just outside the silhouette (`tools/rim.py`), because they vanished on dark rows and
tiles: Mega Drive, PlayStation 2, PlayStation Portable, Atari 2600 and Master System, and of those added later the
Sega CD, 32X, PC Engine CD, Neo Geo, Neo Geo CD, Lynx and Jaguar. ColecoVision (84 %) and Vectrex (76 %) got it by
name: their black outline vanished on the dark spine all the same, and so did the generated SuperGrafx (56 %). The other pictures are
unchanged. `system_art.rs` has a test that fails for a picture that is dark all over and has no rim.

## Other systems

Systems that are not listed here keep the plain coloured card. The SuperGrafx and the Pokémon mini first stayed
without a picture because Commons has no public-domain photo of them; they have one now, generated from text
(above). The arcade folders (CPS1, CPS2, CPS3) have no single console to picture.
